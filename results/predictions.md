# Predictions

Each experiment's prediction, committed before it runs and never edited (criterion 31).
`scripts/check_predictions.py` holds every `results/bench_*.md` to this file and to the
order of the commits. What became of each run is in `results/run_log.md`, failed runs
included.

## P-0001: stub-book harness proof on macOS, 12302019

- Experiment: `bench_replay` on `12302019.NASDAQ_ITCH50`, fresh process per repetition, 3 repetitions, on `macOS (M4 Max)`, through `measure.sh` and `/usr/bin/time -l`.
- What it is for: showing that the harness works on a whole day. The book is the Phase 2 stub, so this says nothing about the book Phase 3 builds.
- Prediction, harness: every page of the input is resident before each timed pass, all 3 repetitions complete, and no pin is claimed.
- Prediction, timer: the quantum is 41.67 ns and the floor 166.67 ns, so p50 and p90 are reported as unresolved.
- Prediction, throughput: between 3 and 15 million messages/s, CV under 5%.
- Prediction, instrumentation: the latency pass takes between 1.0 and 1.6 times the throughput pass.
- Prediction, memory: peak RSS near 9.6 GiB, from the mapped file, 1.0 GiB of samples and the book, and it fits in 64 GB.

## P-0002: pointer chase against the Zen 1 counters on debian

- Experiment: `bench_chase`, 3 fresh processes, 20,000,000 dependent loads per size, one random cycle over every line, 4 KB pages, at 64 KB, 128 KB, 256 KB, 1 MB, 2 MB, 3 MB, 8 MB, 16 MB, 64 MB and 256 MB, on `debian`, through `measure.sh` and `taskset -c 4`.
- What it is for: deciding whether raw `r0843:u` may stand in for last-level misses, and what `cache-misses` counts. `debian` has 512 KB of L2 per core and 4 MB of L3.
- Prediction, well under L2: `r0843:u` gives at most 0.01 fills per load at 64 KB and 128 KB.
- Prediction, far above L3: `r0843:u` gives between 0.90 and 1.10 fills per load at 64 MB and 256 MB.
- Prediction, between L2 and L3: `cache-misses` gives between 0.70 and 1.30 per load at 1 MB, 2 MB and 3 MB, and at each of those sizes it stands at least 0.50 per load above `r0843:u`, so it counts L2 misses and not last-level misses.
- Prediction, the read paths: in every row, for `r0843:u` and `cache-misses`, `rdpmc` and `read()` differ by at most 0.001 per load, and each event's running ratio is 1.0000.
- Prediction, the kernel's mapping: sysfs gives `cache-misses` as `event=0x64,umask=0x09`, and raw `r0964:u`, counted in the same group, stays within 0.001 per load of `cache-misses` in every row.
- All of this holds in each of the 3 runs, not in their median alone. If any of it fails, no last-level number is published, the gap is stated, and the failed run stays in `results/run_log.md`.

## P-0003: the chase with a deliberately wrong event

- Experiment: `bench_chase --raw-event 0x0143`, one process, the same sizes and loads as P-0002, on `debian`, through `measure.sh` and `taskset -c 4`, judged by the same `scripts/chase_report.py`.
- What it is for: showing on real hardware that the verdict can fail. `0x0143` is PMCx043 bit 0, fills from local L2, so it counts the opposite end of the hierarchy. The file stays in the research cache and nothing from it is published.
- Prediction: the verdict is FAIL. The encoding criterion fails, the under-L2 criterion fails with close to one fill per load at 64 KB and 128 KB, and the above-L3 criterion fails with close to none at 64 MB and 256 MB.

- Experiment: `bench_kernels --experiment field --width W` for W in 2, 4, 6 and 8, on `--kernel scalar` (memcpy and `std::byteswap`: a load and BSWAP, or an 8-bit rotate at 2 bytes, with a shift at 6), `--kernel ssse3` and `--kernel avx2` (each an 8-byte load into an XMM register, PSHUFB, MOVQ back to a general register and a shift; AVX2's is the same 128-bit code, VEX-encoded), from the clang 23 release build, through `scripts/simd_driver.py`, each process `~/quant-projects/bin/measure.sh taskset -c 4 bench_kernels ... --core 4` with its output in files on `debian`, `machine_check.sh` on core 4 under measure.sh before and after, 3 repetitions in fresh processes, their order turned each repetition, launched from a non-interactive bash at nice 0. Each process maps the whole of `12302019.NASDAQ_ITCH50`, reads it resident, then walk, work, work, walk, as P-0601. Counters, each over the whole pass, `:u`: cycles, instructions, branch misses, `cache-misses` (L2 misses on Zen 1) and `r0843:u` (a floor on DRAM demand fills). Instruction set of every figure: scalar x86-64 general registers, SSSE3 128-bit, AVX2 (256-bit registers, here 128-bit operations).
- How a winner is called: as P-0601; lower is better, A beats B when A's median is below B's and A's largest repetition below B's smallest; otherwise a tie. AVX2 and NEON are different experiments: no figure here answers a Mac one.
- What was seen before this was written: every Mac result, P-0601 to P-0610 (NOTES.md, "SIMD parsing kernels"); E12's clang 23 x86-64 listings, compiled on the Mac (`results/simd_asm_mac.md`): the SSSE3 and AVX2 `field6` are 8 instructions with 3 on XMM registers, so unlike NEON on arm64 clang does not fold the x86 vector field into scalar code, and scalar's `field6` is 10 with two loads. No `bench_kernels` timing on `debian`. Zen 1 facts used, from Agner Fog's instruction tables, AMD Zen 1 (printed pp. 87 to 96): MOV r,m latency 4 at 2 a cycle; MOV m,r one a cycle; BSWAP r 1 op at 4 a cycle; MOVD/MOVQ between general and XMM registers latency 3, one a cycle; PSHUFB x 1 op on P1 or P2, 2 a cycle; VPSHUFB y 2 ops, one a cycle; VMOVDQU m256,y 2 ops, one every 2 cycles; VPCMPEQQ y 2 ops, one a cycle; PCMPEQQ x 1 op, 2 a cycle. Every field costs under 1.5 cycles an op over the walk, and every variant's branch misses an op within 0.001 of its walk's.
- Prediction, memory: peak RSS between 7.6 and 8.2 GiB, the mapped day counted.

## P-0621: where E3's vector loss goes on debian: one Zen 1 event a process beside the consume pass, a diagnostic

- Experiment: `bench_kernels --experiment consume --kernel K --core 4`, K in scalar, ssse3 and avx2, from the release build P-0611 to P-0619 ran, one process for each event and kernel, each `~/quant-projects/bin/measure.sh taskset -c 4 perf stat -x, -e EVENT:u` with its output in files on `debian`, so that perf's one counter and the bench's own five fit Zen 1's six with no multiplexing. Events: `ex_ret_brn_ind_misp`, `fp_sched_empty`, `de_dis_dispatch_token_stalls0.retire_token_stall`, `.alu_token_stall`, `.agsq_token_stall`, `.alsq3_0_token_stall`, `ls_stlf`, `fpu_pipe_assignment.total` and `stalled-cycles-frontend` (PMCx087 umask 0x02, a fetch-side count on Zen 1). Then one `perf record -e cycles:u` process a kernel and `perf annotate` of its consume loop. Each process runs the work pass three times (the warm-up included) and the walk twice, so a kernel's figure against scalar is the difference of the two counts over three times the day's 263,241,937 ops. A diagnostic for NOTES.md with its raw paths, not a results file.
- What was seen before this was written: P-0613 and P-0614 on `debian`: in E3 SSSE3 took 35.7 and AVX2 41.7 cycles an op against scalar's 23.5, with 41.1, 38.6 and 40.0 instructions, 0.567, 0.567 and 0.565 branch misses and 0.13 to 0.14 L2 misses an op; in E4's whole parse SSSE3 46.3 and AVX2 48.1 cycles a message against scalar's 35.1, branch misses again equal. Agner Fog (microarchitecture, AMD Zen 1-2, p. 237): store forwarding works on Zen 1 in all cases, reads of part of a write included.
- Prediction: on SSSE3 and AVX2 at least 0.3 an op of the branch misses are indirect (`ex_ret_brn_ind_misp`), against under 0.05 on scalar, so the misses are as many but the vector kernels' resolve later, behind the jump table's load. The cycles samples of the vector kernels fall most on the instructions after the indirect jump and on the MOVQ and MOVD transfers.
