## What is timed

Both sides replay the same extract, the 1,519,871 messages of AAPL and every System Event of 12302019.NASDAQ_ITCH50, through the same C++, `include/itch/demo_replay.hpp` on `FastBook<>` at its default capacity, the arm the project ships. Each side runs one warm-up and then 21 timed replays, each on a fresh book built before its span opens. The span is one call of `Replay::replay()`: the frame walk and `FastBook::apply()` for every message, with each status counted. Natively the clock is `mach_absolute_time` around the call, in `bench_demo`; in the browser it is `performance.now()` around the call into the engine, in the page's bench mode, since WebAssembly has no clock of its own. Each side's rate is the extract's messages over its span, and the ratio is the browser's median span over native's. Both left the book with digest `655a813f5bb524dd`, the one `itch_extract` reached when it cut the extract.

The two builds differ in more than the target: native is Clang 23.1.4 for arm64, the browser's engine is em++ for wasm32 with 128-bit SIMD, and a 64-bit pointer becomes a 32-bit one.

No per-message latency is shown: a replay is the only span either side times, and the browser's clock cannot resolve one message.
