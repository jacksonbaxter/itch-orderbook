## T-TRADE-3: `E + C_Y + P` against SEC MIDAS

MIDAS gives thousands of shares to three decimals, as text written from doubles, so a value resolves to one share. ITCH gives whole shares. The parser turns the text into whole shares with no floating point, and refuses a value more than a millionth of a share from a whole number. Every comparison is then integer equality, per symbol.

Pinned window: `0935_1600`, half open, 09:35:00 to 16:00:00 in the feed's own timestamps. It is pinned by measurement: the lead was the SEC's method page for its Market Activity Report, which says its sample runs from 9:35 and does not say that of `TradeVol`. Each cell counts symbols of 5,771 that agree exactly.

An odd lot is a print below the round lot of the SEC's rule, from its method page for the Market Activity Report: "Until Nov 3, 2025, a round lot is defined as 100 shares for all but three tickers. BRK.A and SEB have a round lot of one. BH has a round lot of 10." T-TRADE-3 first failed on odd lots with a fixed 100, on BH and SEB, and then with the directory's lot, on MKL and NVR, which it lists at 10. This rule was chosen after those failures, from the SEC's published text, and nothing was fitted. The page goes on: "All trades involving nine or fewer shares are considered odd lot trades." That adds nothing at a lot of 100 or 10. At a lot of one it is not what MIDAS does, as the last column below shows for SEB, so the check takes a print below the lot. 11 listed symbols have a directory round lot other than 100 this day. Those with a MIDAS row are below, as prints / shares, with what the directory's lot gives kept for information. Every other compared symbol is 100 on both rules.
