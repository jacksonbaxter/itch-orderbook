What these latencies leave out: each is measured from the push, not from the time the message was due. A send held back behind an earlier slow push is stamped only when it goes, so its wait is in no cell above; it shows only in the late count, and there only once it reaches a whole gap, or in burst the next message's due time. In burst, messages that share an ITCH timestamp share a due time, so one is late as soon as a tick passes when the next shares its time, and the count cannot tell that from a wait behind slow pushes. This favours a queue with slow pushes: the slower its pushes, the more of each message's wait falls before its stamp, outside the figures.
